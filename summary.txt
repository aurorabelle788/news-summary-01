World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026. Two days of US-mediated talks between Russia and Ukraine ended in Abu Dhabi with no ceasefire, but agreement on the largest prisoner exchange of the war -- 1,000 to 1,500 on each side, Kyiv said -- as envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff headed to meet Vladimir Putin in Moscow (Reuters, NPR). Russia meanwhile struck six power substations in central Ukraine, and an attack on a market in the Odesa region killed three (Reuters). At COP31 in Antalya, world leaders opened a two-day summit; UN chief Antonio Guterres warned that slow climate action had put the world "on a path to destruction", with 77 heads of state due to speak, down from 116 at COP28, and the US absent for a second year (Reuters). Turkey's presidency targeted electricity at 35% of final energy use by 2035, and five countries backed a plan to double homes with rooftop solar by 2030 (Reuters, Just Security). The US Justice Department charged three Iran-backed operatives over about 20 plots in the US and Europe, including one at RAF Fairford in England, and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent moved to a military base after threats (New York Times). Wall Street rebounded: the S&P 500 rose 1.3% to 7,619, the Nasdaq 1.6% to 25,466 and the Dow 0.5% to 52,486, as CoreWeave jumped 25% on strong earnings and Nvidia rose 3% (Investopedia). Micron rose 12%, gold slipped below $4,000 an ounce and Brent rose to $92.76 a barrel; bonds were shut for Veterans Day (Investopedia, Reuters). The House voted 211-204 to send eight articles impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively blocking the effort (AP). British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer with a NATO unit near Gloucester on suspicion of spying for Russia; Moscow denied any link (CNN, AP). NATO jets were scrambled overnight after at least four drones strayed into Baltic airspace (Just Security). Delhi's air stayed poor at 299 on the air quality index after Diwali (Zark News).
